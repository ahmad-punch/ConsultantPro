# How the business works

This page explains the whole machine in one read. The other pages go deeper on each part.

## The life of a case, stage by stage

A **case** is one piece of work for one client. Every case has a **stage**, which says where it is. The stage names below are exactly what you see in the panel.

**Before a case exists, there is a lead.** A lead is someone who has asked for help but has not agreed to buy. Leads come from the public "Start a case" form, WhatsApp, or a person in a community group. When the person is ready to buy, you press Convert to case on the lead and a case is born.

### 1. Intake

The case has just been opened. We have the person's details and a short description of the problem. Nothing is paid yet. The job: confirm the details with the client and send the payment link. Aim to reply the same working day.

### 2. Triage

A case manager works out three things: the category (property, inheritance, tax, NADRA, and so on), which office or court owns the problem, and how serious it is. Then we recommend the smallest product that answers the client's question, with its price. Aim to finish triage within one working day.

If the request is one we must not take, such as a request to get around the official process, the case is set to **Declined** with the reason written in a note.

### 3. Memo drafting

For questions, calls and assessments, and for managed cases, this is where the written answer or **Pathway Memo** is prepared. The case manager writes it in a Google Doc inside the client's Drive folder. An independent partner advocate reviews the legal content. The target is a memo within 5 working days of the case being opened.

### 4. Memo delivered

The memo has gone to the client. The button Mark memo delivered stamps the date. That date is used to measure how fast we are. If the client wants more, the case may move on to Managed. If not, it moves to Closed.

### 5. Managed

Only for the Managed Case product. The client has hired an independent advocate directly, and we project-manage: documents, scheduling, errands, chasing, and a written update at every milestone and at least every 14 days, even if nothing has moved.

### 6. Errand in progress

Only for the Errand product. An errand runner is visiting the government office. Errands skip the memo stages: Intake, Triage, Errand in progress, Closed.

### 7. Closed

The work is finished. We hand back all documents, write a closing note, and ask the client two questions: how likely they are to recommend us from 0 to 10 (this is the **NPS** score), and whether they would recommend us at all. We record both with Close out. These answers feed one of the six gate numbers.

### 8. Declined

A side exit available from any stage. We turned the work away and wrote why. It is not a failure to be hidden. It is how we protect the business.

The stage can be changed in two ways: Advance stage moves the case one step along its normal path, and Set stage jumps anywhere, with a written reason.

## Who does what

- **Founders.** Sales, intake, case management and choosing suppliers in the early months. They approve content and read the weekly numbers.
- **Case manager.** Owns a case from intake to close. Is the client's single contact. Drafts memos, writes updates, chases partners.
- **Operations.** Keeps the system tidy: dispatches errands, schedules calls, checks templates and deadlines.
- **Partner advocate.** An independent lawyer. Reviews the legal content of memos, advises the client, represents them. Writes on their own letterhead and **bills the client directly**.
- **Errand runner.** A person on the ground who visits government offices for a fixed fee.
- **Ambassador.** A respected community member who recommends us and earns a commission on paid cases they brought.
- **Adviser.** An experienced professional who reviews our playbooks and lends credibility, only when they have agreed to be named.
- **Accountant.** A tax practitioner we use for filer and tax cases.

Only founders, case managers and operations staff are users of the panel. Partners, ambassadors and advisers are records in it, not users.

## How money flows

There are three separate streams. They must never be mixed.

1. **Our fee.** The price on our list. The client pays us by Stripe link, Wise, Payoneer or bank transfer. We record it under Payments. For managed cases we charge in stages, so the client never prepays more than the next stage.
2. **The advocate's fee.** Agreed between the client and the advocate. The client pays the advocate directly. We never collect it and we take no share. This is a legal rule about advocates.
3. **Government fees.** Court, stamp and office fees, charged at cost with the official receipt. We add nothing on top.

Two smaller flows:

- **Ambassador commission.** A percentage of the price of paid cases linked to the ambassador. We pay it ourselves and record it with Record payout.
- **Errand runner pay.** Part of the errand fee, agreed with the runner.

Refund promise, in plain words: if we miss the memo deadline on a Case Assessment, the client gets that fee back. If a client cancels before we start a stage, that stage is refunded. ## The 90-day test

We are testing an idea, not running a finished company. The rule is to build no new product and sell by hand for 90 days. At the end we decide with numbers. The six numbers, all shown on the Metrics screen:

1. **Paid cases.** At least 20.
2. **Revenue per paid interaction.** At least $40 on average.
3. **Referrals.** At least 30 percent of paid cases come from an ambassador or an earlier client.
4. **Retainer sign-ups.** At least 3.
5. **Median days to memo.** Under 5 working days.
6. **Would recommend.** At least 8 in 10 clients who answered.

If all six pass within 60 days of the first sale, the answer is go. If fewer than 10 paid cases come from reaching 500 or more people, or partners cannot keep to fixed fees and deadlines, the answer is no-go. A mixed result gets one more 30-day round with a single change. The decision is written down in the decisions folder.

Targets in the plan are assumptions. They are what we are testing.
