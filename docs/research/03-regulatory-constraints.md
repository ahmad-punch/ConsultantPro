# Regulatory Constraints on a Serving Police Officer (and Spouse, a Civil Servant) Backing a Paid Advisory/Concierge Business for Overseas Pakistanis

**Prepared:** 30 September 2026
**Scope:** Legality and risk of the proposed structure — a serving SP/ASP-rank police officer (CSP/PSP cadre) and his serving-civil-servant spouse as "behind the scenes" owner-advisers of a paid advisory/concierge company serving overseas Pakistanis (police/FIR help, courts, land, tax, NADRA, company registration, errands), with lawyer relatives as the public/professional face.

**Bottom line up front:** The core problem is not really "legal tech" or "startup structuring" — it is that **a serving police officer taking money (directly or through a company he owns/controls) for influence, advice, or facilitation on matters touching police/court business is close to the paradigm fact-pattern of Prevention of Corruption Act s.5(1)(d), PPC ss.161–165, and NAB Ordinance s.9**, layered on top of an unconditional Conduct Rules bar on undertaking any private trade, business, or company directorship without prior government sanction (which will essentially never be granted for this). Every structuring option below is a way of managing that core problem, not eliminating it, unless the officer takes zero economic interest and zero operational role, ever, in anything that touches police, FIR, or prosecutorial matters.

## 1. Government Servants (Conduct) Rules 1964 / provincial equivalents, Police Order 2002, Civil Servants Act 1973, and Establishment Division practice

### 1.1 The federal Conduct Rules — verbatim text

The **Government Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1964** (5 June 1964, made under Art. 178/179 of the Constitution) apply to all persons serving the Federal Government and to All-Pakistan Service officers (which includes PSP/CSP-cadre police officers) even while serving under provincial governments (Rule 2). Full text: [paklawyer.com PDF](https://paklawyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Government-Servants-Conduct-Rules-1964-1.pdf).

- **Rule 15 — Promotion and management of companies, etc.**
  > "No Government servant shall, except with the previous sanction of the Government, take part in the promotion, registration or management of any bank or company: Provided that a Government servant may, subject to the provisions of any general or special order of the Government, take part in the promotion, registration or management of a Cooperative Society…"

  This directly bars the officer from being a promoter, director, or manager of the proposed company without prior government sanction. There is no carve-out for a "sleeping"/passive shareholding — the rule catches promotion and registration, not just active management, so simply being named as a subscriber/director in the SECP incorporation papers is itself the prohibited act absent sanction.

- **Rule 16 — Private trade, employment or work**
  > "(1) No Government servant shall, except with the previous sanction of the Government, engage in any trade or undertake any employment or work, other than his official duties: Provided that he may, without such sanction, undertake honorary work of a religious, social or charitable nature or occasional work of a literary or artistic character, subject to conditions that his official duties do not thereby suffer and that the occupation or undertaking does not conflict or is not inconsistent with the position or obligations as a Government servant… (2) …no Government servant shall associate himself with any private trust, foundation or similar other institution which is not sponsored by the Government."

  A paid advisory business is squarely "trade… or work" — not honorary, not literary/artistic — so it requires prior sanction. Given the subject matter (advice on FIRs, police conduct, courts) directly overlaps his own department's business, sanction would in practice be refused; conflict-of-interest is baked into the rule ("does not conflict… with the position… as a Government servant").

- **Rule 14 — Speculation and investment**
  > "(2) No Government servant shall make, or permit any member of his family to make, any investment likely to embarrass or influence him in the discharge of his official duties. (3) No Government servant shall make any investment the value of which is likely to be affected by some event of which information is available to him as a Government servant and is not equally available to the general public."

  Even a passive equity stake by the officer (let alone active promotion) in a company whose value depends on his own official conduct/reputation is the kind of "investment likely to… influence him" the rule targets.

- **Rule 12/13 — Declaration of immovable property / disclosure of assets**: any shareholding, if structured as the officer's own asset, must be declared; concealment (e.g., via a benami/nominee arrangement) is itself an additional offence (see NAB s.9 and s.5-C of the Prevention of Corruption Act on disproportionate/undeclared assets, §2 below).
- **Rule 20/21/22 — Newspapers, radio broadcasts, publications, public statements**: any media-facing or PR role for the officer (blog posts, "ask the cop" content, public branding) needs prior sanction too.
- Rule 3(1)(c) defines "member of a Government servant's family" to include the wife — relevant because several of the rules (14(2), 26 "nepotism… wilful abuse of office") reach conduct by, or for the benefit of, a spouse.

There is no rule in the 1964 text that expressly targets "spouse's business" by name, but Rule 14(2) (investments that could embarrass/influence the officer) and Rule 26 (nepotism/favouritism/wilful abuse of office) functionally do the same job when the spouse's business is fed by, or trades on, the officer's police position.

### 1.2 A live, on-point precedent: FBR's 2022 bar on private consultancy by its own officers

This is the single most directly analogous piece of evidence found, and it is a **real enforcement circular, not a hypothetical**:

> "The Honorable FTO initiated an Own Motion Investigation regarding private practice by the employees of FBR who joined local chambers or even opened their own law offices and render legal assistance to taxpayers in the evenings or even during office hours. Findings were recorded that many of the officers/officials of FBR associate themselves with different taxpayers and provide legal assistance to them in various taxation matters in total disregard of the instructions under the **Government Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1964** whereunder no government servant is allowed to engage in any trade or undertake any employment or work, other than his official duties, except with the previous sanction of the government. Furthermore, **Estt. Div.'s O.M. No.1/20/76-D-IV dated 6-3-1976** contains clear prohibitions regarding undertaking of private work by the government servants… Chairman, FBR has taken a serious view of the matter and all FBR employees are again advised to completely abstain themselves from private consultancy/tax practice… if any officer/official is found involved in such practice, strict disciplinary action shall be taken under **Civil Servants (E&D) Rules, 2020**."
> — FBR Circular C.No.13(5)/S-MIR-I/2021/112843-R, 28 April 2022, ["Bar Against Indulging in Private Consultancy/Tax Practice by Officials of FBR"](https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/Docs/202243094332733PrivateConsultancyTaxPracticeOfficialsFBR.pdf)

Key takeaways for this project:
- The Establishment Division has a standing, decades-old office memorandum (O.M. No.1/20/76-D-IV, 6 March 1976) generically prohibiting government servants from private consultancy work — this is not FBR-specific.
- Officers who quietly moonlighted as consultants/advocates for people dealing with their own department were treated as a disciplinary problem serious enough to trigger an Ombudsman investigation and department-wide circulars, with express reference to **Civil Servants (E&D) Rules 2020** (dismissal/removal-track rules) as the enforcement mechanism.
- The proposed business is structurally the same pattern: a serving officer (police, not FBR) whose company advises the public on matters that fall within his own institution's jurisdiction (FIRs, police complaints) — just one step removed via ownership rather than direct practice.

### 1.3 Provincial Conduct Rules (Punjab/Sindh/KP)

The provincial rules for civil servants (which cover provincially-recruited police officers; PSP/CSP-cadre officers are federal All-Pakistan Service officers subject to the *federal* 1964 rules per Rule 2's extension) are near-identical copies of the 1964 federal rules:

- **Punjab Civil Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1966** — full text: [regulationswing.punjab.gov.pk PDF](https://regulationswing.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/08_Pb_Civil_Servants_Conduct_Rules_1966.pdf). Punjab's own commentary on these rules records specific concern about spouses: "the government has noted that wives of some Government servants have been engaging in trade and business where the husband's influence could possibly be misused," with limited exceptions for things like poultry/dairy farming, and a requirement (Rule 12) that a civil servant's asset declaration include income earned by the spouse. [Source discussion](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/conduct-rules-1966/68222802) — **unverified** as to the exact current rule number/text since the source is a secondary slide deck rather than the gazetted text itself, but consistent with the federal Rule 14(2)/26 logic above.
- **Sindh Government Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1966**: same Rule 15/16 language — "No Government servant shall, except with the previous sanction of Government, engage in any trade or undertake, any employment or work, other than his official duties" and the identical bank/company promotion bar, substituting the Sindh Cooperative Societies Act, 1925 ([Sindh Civil Servants Act 1973](https://sstsindh.gov.pk/admin/upload/rules/THE%20SINDH%20CIVIL%20SERVANTS%20ACT,%201973.pdf) is the parent statute; conduct-rule text confirmed via search extract, **not independently PDF-verified — treat rule number as indicative, not exact**).
- KP equivalent rules were not independently located in this research pass; on the pattern of Punjab/Sindh they should be assumed materially identical. **Unverified.**

### 1.4 Civil Servants Act 1973

The Act (federal) is the parent statute under which the Conduct Rules are made. Relevant, generally-stated provisions (confirmed via search extract of [pakistancode.gov.pk text](https://pakistancode.gov.pk/english/UY2FqaJw1-apaUY2Fqa-apaUY2NqZpY=-sg-jjjjjjjjjjjjj), not independently PDF-verified line-by-line):
- Conduct of a civil servant "shall be regulated by rules made, or instructions issued, by Government" — i.e., the Act delegates to the 1964 Conduct Rules and Establishment Division OMs described above.
- Private employment is explicitly permitted **only** during leave preparatory to retirement or after retirement, and even then requires prior approval of the prescribed authority if sought within two years of retirement. This confirms, a contrario, that private employment/business during active service is not a statutory entitlement subject to disclosure — it needs affirmative sanction under the Conduct Rules, and is closed off almost entirely for a serving officer.

### 1.5 Police Order 2002 and Police Rules 1934

- **Police Order 2002, Article 117 ("Police Officer not to engage in any other employment")**, in Chapter XII ("Regulation, Control and Discipline of the Police"): confirmed by table-of-contents extraction from the consolidated text at [manzoorlawbook.com](https://manzoorlawbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/contents-of-Police-Order.-lfwfu6.pdf) and cross-checked against an MCQ bank quoting the operative sentence: "No Police Officer shall engage in any private employment while he is a member of the police establishment." ([mcqtimes.com](https://mcqtimes.com/no-police-officer-shall-engage-in-any-private-employment-while-he-is-a-member-of-the-police-establishment-as-provided-by-article-_________-of-the-police-order-2002/)) This is a **service-specific, sector-specific overlay on top of** the general Conduct Rules bar — it applies to the officer as a police officer regardless of cadre, and Chapter XII (Articles 112–117) also empowers rule-making on discipline and lists misconduct offences (Article 155, "Penalty for certain types of misconduct by Police Officers") that would capture undisclosed business dealings connected to police work.
- **Police Rules 1934**: full text could not be extracted as readable content in this research pass (PDF encoding issues across every source tried); its administrative/discipline provisions are understood to work alongside Police Order 2002 rather than independently addressing private business, but this could not be verified against primary text in this session. **Unverified — recommend a follow-up pull of the Punjab Police Rules 1934 PDF specifically for Chapter on "Discipline" if this needs a citable quote.**

### 1.6 Practical conclusion for §1

There is no plausible reading under which a serving CSP/PSP officer can lawfully be:
- a shareholder/director/promoter of the company (Rule 15, Conduct Rules) without prior government sanction; or
- paid for "trade… or work" of this kind (Rule 16) without prior sanction; or
- exempt from Police Order 2002 Art. 117's blanket "not to engage in any other employment" bar (which does not even have Rule 16's honorary-work carve-out).

Sanction is a real, obtainable-in-theory mechanism (it is how, e.g., a serving officer might be permitted to write a book or hold a directorship in a purely charitable trust), but it is granted by the officer's own department/Establishment Division on a case-by-case basis, is discretionary, and — for a business whose value proposition is literally "connections and know-how inside the police/court system" — sanction would very likely be refused if honestly applied for, and the act of applying would itself flag the venture to the department. Proceeding without applying, and hoping to stay "behind the scenes," is the disciplinary offence the FBR circular describes, not a loophole around it.

## 2. Conflict of interest / corruption exposure

### 2.1 Prevention of Corruption Act 1947 — s.5, "criminal misconduct"

Full relevant text (confirmed from [ace.punjab.gov.pk PDF](https://ace.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/THE_PREVENTION_OF_CORRUPTION_ACT_1947.pdf), Anti-Corruption Establishment Punjab's own guide book):

> "A public servant is said to commit the offence of criminal misconduct— … (d) If he, by corrupt or illegal means, or by otherwise abusing his position as public servant, obtains for himself or for any other person any valuable thing or pecuniary advantage… Any public servant who commits or attempts to commit criminal misconduct… shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to seven years or with fine or with both."

Clause (d) does **not** require proof of a specific corrupt transaction linked to a specific official act — "by otherwise abusing his position as public servant" is broad enough to cover an officer whose company profits *because* the public perceives (correctly) that having "his" company handle your FIR/police matter gets preferential treatment, even where no individual bribe changes hands. Clause (b) is also relevant: obtaining "any valuable thing without consideration or for a consideration which he knows to be inadequate, from any person whom he knows to have been or to be, or to be likely to be concerned in any proceeding or business transacted by him" — i.e., taking a paying client whose *own* case might one day cross the officer's desk, or who is trying to buy goodwill for a future case, fits this clause almost exactly. Section 5-C separately criminalises possession of property/pecuniary resources disproportionate to known income (relevant if the officer's shareholding/dividends are not declared).

### 2.2 Pakistan Penal Code ss.161–165 (Chapter IX, offences by/relating to public servants)

Confirmed section headings and gratification-related text (search-extracted, s.161 verbatim-confirmed via [pljlawsite.com](http://www.pljlawsite.com/html/ppc161.htm); full verbatim text of 162–165 could not be pulled from a working online source in this session despite multiple attempts — **treat 162–165 wording below as summary, not verbatim, pending a follow-up pull of a clean PPC PDF**):

- **s.161** (verbatim): "Whoever being or expecting to be a public servant, accepts or obtains, or agrees to accept, or attempts to obtain from any person, for himself or for any other person, any gratification whatever, other than legal remuneration, as a motive or reward for doing or forbearing to do any official act… shall be punished with imprisonment… which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both."
- **s.162** — taking gratification, by corrupt or illegal means, to influence a public servant.
- **s.163** — taking gratification, without corrupt/illegal means, to exercise personal influence with a public servant (this is the closest fit to "we can get your FIR/complaint handled because our co-founder is a serving SP" — it does not even require the influence to be exercised corruptly, only that money changed hands for the promise of personal influence).
- **s.164** — punishment for abetment by a public servant of offences under s.162/163.
- **s.165** — a public servant obtaining any valuable thing, without consideration or for inadequate consideration, from a person concerned in a proceeding or business transacted by that public servant.
- Punishment under ss.161–163/165/165-A: imprisonment up to three years, or fine, or both. (search-extracted summary; **cross-check exact terms against a clean PPC text before using in any client-facing legal memo**.)

The Prevention of Corruption Act 1947 s.3 makes offences under ss.161–165 *cognizable* (police can arrest/investigate without a magistrate's prior order in most circumstances, subject to s.5-A's rank-of-investigating-officer safeguard), and s.4 creates a reverse presumption of guilt once acceptance of gratification is shown, unless the accused rebuts it. This materially raises the stakes: even a single disgruntled "client" alleging that the company's success was really about paying for the officer's help is enough to trigger a cognizable corruption case with a presumption running against the accused.

### 2.3 NAB Ordinance 1999, s.9 — "misuse of authority"

Confirmed via search extract of s.9 text (attempts to pull the full clean text of s.9 from pakistancode.gov.pk and nasirlawsite.com were not completed in this session — **treat as summary, verify before drafting**):

> s.9(a)(vi): "if he intentionally misuses his authority by dis-regarding law so as to gain any monetary benefit or favour for himself or any other person related to him or on his behalf." 
> s.9(a)(iv): "if he by corrupt, dishonest, or illegal means, obtains or, seeks to obtain for himself, or for his spouse or dependents or any other person, any property, valuable things or pecuniary advantage."

Both clauses expressly reach benefit flowing to a "person related to him" or "his spouse" — i.e., structuring the business around the *spouse* as nominal owner, with the officer merely "advising," does **not** avoid s.9 exposure if the officer's authority/position is what generates the business's value. NAB's threshold and referral practice have been narrowed by the 2022 NAB amendments (raising monetary thresholds, largely excluding routine government business decisions, and requiring the accountability court to weigh certain factors) — this is a real and significant mitigant for garden-variety civil servants, but the amendments were principally aimed at protecting policy/procurement decision-makers from being second-guessed after the fact; they do not obviously immunise an officer who has taken a private ownership stake in a company that trades on his police authority. **Unverified in detail — recommend a lawyer confirm current NAB monetary/eligibility thresholds before relying on "NAB amendments protect me" as a risk mitigant.**

### 2.4 Is this realistically "influence peddling"/"touting" as a matter of substance, not just theory?

Yes — the business model as described (a company staffed/owned by a serving SP-rank officer and reachable via his practising-lawyer relatives, marketed to overseas Pakistanis specifically for police/FIR/court problems) is close to the textbook definition of influence-peddling: it monetises the *appearance and reality* of an inside track with the police and judicial system. Two independent, real enforcement patterns confirm regulators and the department itself treat this pattern seriously:
- FBR's 2022 circular (above) treating even *tax* consultancy by tax officials as a disciplinary emergency;
- FIA's own recent internal discipline record, including **dismissal of a Sub-Inspector for "corruption, incompetence and employing a private individual for official work"** ([24newshd.tv, 29-Sep-2026](https://www.24newshd.tv/29-Sep-2026/fia-officer-dismissed-corruption-use-private-clerk)) and a broader round of "76 departmental punishments, including dismissal of 10 employees and demotion of 8" at FIA Islamabad Zone ([nation.com.pk, 26-Sep-2026](https://www.nation.com.pk/26-Sep-2026/fia-takes-major-disciplinary-action-10-dismissed-8-demoted-among-76-punished)) — showing that even indirect delegation of official functions to private individuals is being actively punished in the current environment (late 2026), not a dead-letter rule.

**This is the single highest-risk element of the whole plan and should be treated as effectively prohibited, full stop, for as long as the officer is serving**, regardless of how the company's cap table is drawn up.

## 3. Legal profession rules — can the company sell "legal advice," and how do platforms like Wukla structure around this?

### 3.1 Legal Practitioners and Bar Councils Act 1973 / Legal Practitioners Act 1879

- The **Legal Practitioners Act 1879** has been almost entirely repealed over time (by the Advocates Act 1961 and, as recently as the **Advocates (Amendment) Act 2023**, effective 30-9-2024) — confirmed by reading the full arrangement-of-sections table at [juris-codex.com](https://juris-codex.com/act_pdfs/Legal_Practitioners_Act_1879.pdf): every substantive section (1–42) is marked "[Repealed.]", including **s.36, "Power to frame and publish lists of touts,"** which was itself repealed by the Advocates (Amendment) Act 2023. **This is an important, non-obvious finding**: the "tout" machinery under the 1879 Act (definition of tout, court-maintained tout lists, up to 3 years' imprisonment for touting) that older secondary sources describe is **no longer in force** in Pakistan in its 1879 form — regulation of touting/solicitation has effectively migrated to the **1973 Act and the 1976 Bar Council Rules' Canons of Professional Conduct** instead. Any advice built on "the Legal Practitioners Act 1879 tout provisions" should be treated as historical background, not current operative law, absent confirmation that some other Act preserved the substance.
- **Legal Practitioners and Bar Councils Act, 1973 (Act XXXV of 1973)** is the current parent statute establishing the Bar Councils and their disciplinary jurisdiction over advocates. [Text](https://pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administrator4068b39bc9eebedce40baf56a335243d.pdf).

### 3.2 Pakistan Legal Practitioners and Bar Councils Rules 1976, Chapter XII — Canons of Professional Conduct and Etiquette

Search-extracted text (from a Studocu/Scribd transcription of the official rules; **not independently PDF-verified against the gazetted rules in this session — treat rule numbers as indicative**):

- **Rule 135 (advertising/solicitation):** "An advocate shall not solicit professional employment by advertisement or by any other means. However, this clause shall not be construed as prohibiting the publication or use of ordinary professional cards, name plates or conventional listing in directories, so long as the information contained therein is limited to professional and academic qualifications, and public offices currently held, and does not contain any matter which savours of personal advertisement."
- **Rule 136:** an advocate shall not employ any other person to solicit or obtain professional employment on the advocate's behalf.
- **Touting/fee-sharing standard** (as commonly restated in Pakistani legal-ethics teaching materials, tracking the position also taken in Indian/Malaysian bar rules on the same colonial-era model): "The acceptance by an Advocate of any form of payment (such as commission) from any person merely for introducing clients without the provision of legal services by the introducer, is tantamount to touting"; conversely, genuine fee-splitting between advocates who both actually render legal services is permitted, but **paying a non-lawyer company/platform a fee or commission for referring clients to the advocate is not** — this is the operative constraint on how a "concierge" company can lawfully interact with the lawyer relatives.
- Non-observance of Chapter XII "shall be deemed to be professional misconduct making [the advocate] liable for disciplinary action" — i.e., enforcement runs against the **advocate**, not the company, but a company built around inducing that misconduct (e.g., paying the lawyer-relatives a cut of referral revenue) puts the lawyers' licences at risk, which is a second-order but real business risk (your professional "face" gets struck off).

### 3.3 What this means for the product

- The company **cannot itself "sell legal advice"** — only a licensed advocate can give legal advice/opinions and appear in matters; a company employing/paying advocates to do so, or splitting legal fees with them as a business partner (rather than the advocate simply being retained by the client on ordinary professional terms), risks both (a) unauthorized-practice-style objections from the Bar Council against the company's PR/marketing (there is no clean "corporate practice of law" prohibition on the books equivalent to the US model, but the advocate-side rules on referral fees and fee-splitting bite hard on the business model), and (b) the advocate relatives' own professional discipline.
- The safer, and commercially standard, structure — used by essentially every Pakistani legal-tech platform — is: the **company sells "information," "case tracking," "document preparation," and "referral/concierge" services**, and **separately, transparently connects the client to an independent advocate who bills the client directly** for actual legal advice/representation, with **no revenue share or commission flowing from the advocate's fee back to the company** (a flat, disclosed referral/administrative fee paid by the *client*, not skimmed from the advocate's fee, is the more defensible model; even that carries some Rule 135/136 risk and should be run past a practising Bar Council member before launch).

### 3.4 How Wukla (a real Pakistani legal-tech precedent) is structured

Wukla (Karachi-based, founded by Mubariz Siddiqui, Sohaib Saleem and Altaf Qureshi — one is a practising advocate with an LLM) launched with two clearly separated product lines: **"Ask a Wakil"** (a lawyer-directory/referral tool connecting users to independent advocates who answer queries) and **"Create Agreements"** (templated document generation, delivered to the user, with no advocate appearing as author-of-record on a bespoke opinion). [dawn.com](https://www.dawn.com/news/1278150), [techjuice.pk](https://www.techjuice.pk/wukla-is-pakistans-first-website-for-online-legal-solutions-and-documents-delivery/), [wukla.com legaltech strategy](https://wukla.com/legaltech-strategy/). This is exactly the separation the Canons require: the platform curates/facilitates, the advocate advises and is paid for advising, and the platform's own revenue comes from technology/access fees and templated-document sales rather than a cut of legal fees. **The proposed business should copy this pattern closely** — and, given lawyer relatives are already involved, this is a very achievable structure.

## 4. Company, tax, and payments structuring

### 4.1 SECP entity choice

- **Single Member Company (SMC – Private Limited)**: one shareholder, one director, simplified governance (no mandatory board meetings, no mandatory audit unless paid-up capital exceeds Rs 1 million), but must nominate a successor-in-shares. Converts to a normal Private Limited if a second shareholder joins.
- **Private Limited Company**: 2–50 shareholders, minimum two directors, mandatory annual audit.
[globallawexperts.com](https://globallawexperts.com/how-to-register-a-company-in-pakistan-2026/), [jzarrfilers.com](https://jzarrfilers.com/blog/single-member-company-registration/), [saeedlawfirm.com](https://saeedlawfirm.com/blog/company-registration-in-pakistan-secp).

**Practical point specific to this case:** given the officer legally cannot be a promoter/director/shareholder of any company without government sanction (§1 above), the SECP-level choice between SMC and Pvt Ltd is secondary to the more fundamental question of *whose name(s)* go on the incorporation papers at all. If the spouse and lawyer relatives are the only shareholders/directors of record, a normal Pvt Ltd (so that shares can be split between the spouse and relatives, and so a clean cap table exists if the officer is ever asked to disclose "no interest") is more defensible than an SMC with the spouse as sole member, which invites the "who really controls this" question.

### 4.2 Foreign holding company (UK/US/UAE) — is this normal, and why

Yes, very common for Pakistani-founded startups, for two independent reasons, both confirmed:
1. **Payment-rail access**: "the best payment gateways (like Stripe) are not available in Pakistan" for Pakistan-incorporated entities; a Delaware LLC/C-Corp or UK Ltd can open a US/UK bank account and a Stripe/PayPal merchant account directly. [xpezia.com.pk](https://www.xpezia.com.pk/blog/7-easy-steps-for-pakistanis-to-start-a-us-company-and-unlock-stripe-paypal/)
2. **Investor/legal-form expectations**: US/international investors are comfortable with Delaware C-Corp paper (standardised SAFE/preferred-stock templates, Delaware Chancery Court); many Pakistani startups either incorporate a Delaware/UK parent from day one or "flip" a Pakistani Pvt Ltd into a new foreign holding company via a share exchange once they raise from foreign investors. [globallawexperts.com](https://globallawexperts.com/incorporate-in-pakistan-vs-delaware-for-startups-2026/), [Medium — Mubariz Siddiqui (Wukla co-founder), "Pakistani Startups' Guide to Offshore Holding Companies"](https://mubariz.medium.com/pakistani-startups-guide-to-offshore-holding-companies-c96feac8fde5).

**For this business specifically**, a foreign (UK is generally simpler/cheaper than US for a non-VC-backed services business; UAE free-zone is a third common option for MENA-facing service exporters) holding/operating entity is attractive less for investor optics and more because it (a) makes Stripe/PayPal available for card payments from the diaspora, and (b) puts a layer of distance between the Pakistan-resident operating entity (where the spouse/relatives sit) and the customer-facing brand/payment entity. But it does **not** solve the Conduct Rules or corruption-law problem if the officer holds *any* beneficial interest anywhere in the group, foreign or domestic — Rule 14 (investment) and NAB s.9 are not jurisdictionally limited to Pakistani companies, and a foreign shareholding is, if anything, a worse disclosure story if it ever surfaces (undeclared foreign assets bring FBR's offshore-assets/black-money provisions and NAB's asset-recovery apparatus into play as well). **If a foreign holdco is used, it must exclude the officer from its cap table as completely as the domestic entity does.**

### 4.3 PSEB registration and the FBR IT/ITeS 0.25% final-tax export regime

- PSEB registration is open to Pvt Ltd/SMC companies and even individual freelancers with export clients; it is the gateway to the FBR concessional regime. [urcapk.com](https://urcapk.com/taxation/pakistan-it-exports-pseb-registration-025-tax-rate/), [paktaxcalculator.pk](https://paktaxcalculator.pk/pseb-freelancer-registration/).
- **Current rate**: a 0.25% final withholding tax on IT/ITeS export proceeds, **recently extended through Tax Year 2029** (per the search-extracted secondary sources; the underlying legal basis is s.154A of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 and related SROs — **not independently pulled from FBR's own SRO text in this session; verify the exact clause/rate before relying on it commercially**). [ezinvoice.pk](https://ezinvoice.pk/blog/it-export-tax-rate-extended-2029-pakistan), [taxwizard.pk](https://taxwizard.pk/blog/how-to-claim-it-export-tax-pakistan-2026/).
- **Important caveat for this business**: PSEB's own eligible-activity list (software houses, web dev, BPO/KPO/call-centre, data processing, SaaS, fintech) is broad enough that a **digitally-delivered concierge/advisory platform can plausibly be framed as a KPO ("knowledge process outsourcing") / customer-support ITeS business** — but a company whose real activity is "connect overseas Pakistanis with lawyers and fixers for FIRs and land disputes" is not obviously "IT-enabled services" in the way Upwork-style freelancing or call-centre outsourcing is. This qualification should not be assumed; it should be confirmed with PSEB/a tax adviser before pricing the business model around the 0.25% rate, because if PSEB/FBR later disputes the classification, the company could face full corporate tax rates plus penalties retroactively.

### 4.4 Accepting payments from overseas Pakistanis (UK/US/UAE/Gulf diaspora)

Given Stripe does not onboard Pakistan-registered merchants:
- **Payoneer**: widely used, multi-currency, well-supported payout rail into Pakistan; frequently the payout destination even for other gateways/MoRs. [payoneer.com](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/business/guide-to-payment-gateways-in-pakistan/)
- **Wise**: multi-currency account/transfer rail, commonly used alongside Payoneer.
- **PayFast, Safepay**: Pakistan-based gateways, but "primarily optimized for domestic PKR processing"; useful for the PKR/local-services leg (errands, courier-style tasks paid by resident relatives) rather than the primary GBP/USD/AED diaspora-payment leg.
- **Paddle / Lemon Squeezy as Merchant of Record (MoR)**: the MoR model (Paddle's 2026 headline pricing "~5% + $0.50/transaction, no monthly fee, bundles processing, tax handling, and chargeback management") lets a Pakistan-based team sell to card-paying customers abroad without needing their own Stripe account at all, with payout via wire or Payoneer. [jawadhassan.dev](https://jawadhassan.dev/blog/paddle-vs-lemonsqueezy-pakistan)
- **A UK/US entity with its own Stripe account** (§4.2) remains the lowest-friction, highest-conversion option if the founders are willing to run the payment/legal entity offshore and repatriate funds to the Pakistan operating company as an intercompany service fee (structured and invoiced correctly, and reported to SBP/FBR as required — see §4.5).

None of these carry police-officer-specific risk; this is an ordinary structuring question for any Pakistani service-export startup, independent of the conflict-of-interest problem in §1–2.

### 4.5 SBP rules on receiving service-export proceeds

Significant, recent (2025–2026) liberalisation directly relevant here, confirmed via multiple 2025–2026 news sources:
- IT/ITeS exporters and freelancers **no longer need Form 'R' for every transaction under $25,000**; a one-time declaration at account opening replaces per-transaction filing. [brecorder.com](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40415048/it-firms-freelancers-exempted-from-declaring-export-proceeds-of-up-to-25000)
- Export proceeds/ESFCA transfers are now processed within **one working day**. [nextgen.pk](https://nextgen.pk/news/sbp-abolishes-form-r-it-exports-esfca-dollar-retention-2026)
- Exporters can **retain $5,000/month or 50% of export proceeds (whichever is higher)** in an Exporters' Special Foreign Currency Account (ESFCA) rather than being forced to convert 100% to PKR. [lcci.pk](https://lcci.pk/sbps-new-rules-for-freelancers-get-paid-in-1-day-skip-per-transaction-forms-more/)
- Revised ePRC/S-PRC (Proceed Realization Certificate) formats took effect **1 October 2025**, specifically to make it easier for IT exporters and freelancers to document that foreign funds were properly repatriated. [brecorder.com](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40368328/it-exporters-freelancers-sbp-eases-reporting-formats)

**Caveat**: these liberalised rules are explicitly framed around "IT exporters and freelancers" — the same classification uncertainty flagged in §4.3 (is a concierge/advisory service "IT/ITeS export" for SBP purposes too, or does it default to the general, more paperwork-heavy services-export regime?) applies here as well. **Unverified for this specific business model — confirm with an authorized dealer bank before assuming ESFCA/Form-R relief applies.**

## 5. Data/privacy and liability

- **PECA 2016** (Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act): s.3 criminalises unauthorised access to any information system/data with dishonest intent (up to 3 months/Rs 50,000 fine); s.6 addresses unauthorised access to critical infrastructure; **s.20 ("Offences against dignity of a natural person")** criminalises intentionally, publicly transmitting information known to be false that intimidates or harms a natural person's reputation or privacy (up to 3 years / Rs 1 million fine). [pakistancode.gov.pk](https://www.pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administrator6a061efe0ed5bd153fa8b79b8eb4cba7.pdf), [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_Electronic_Crimes_Act,_2016_%28Pakistan%29). Relevance to this business: the company will be handling clients' CNIC numbers, FIR details, land records, and family/immigration information for overseas Pakistanis — s.20 and the unauthorised-access provisions mean the company must treat this data with real security discipline (encrypted storage, access logging, staff NDAs), because a data breach or a staff member misusing client data is a PECA-cognizable criminal matter, not just a reputational problem.
- **Personal Data Protection Bill**: still not law as of September 2026. The **Personal Data Protection Bill, 2023** completed consultation but was not passed by either house; a further **"Personal Data Protection Act, 2025" draft** has circulated with new compliance obligations for local/international businesses while keeping state agencies largely exempt, but **as of the latest available reporting it has not passed the National Assembly or Senate**. [thefridaytimes.com (Dec 2025)](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/11-Dec-2025/data-privacy-peril-pakistan-s-legal-vacuum-consequences), [chambers.com Data Protection & Privacy 2026 guide](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/pakistan), [genderit.org](https://www.genderit.org/articles/between-privacy-and-power-fine-line-pakistans-data-protection-bill). **Practical effect**: Pakistan currently has no comprehensive statutory data-protection regime with a regulator/fines for ordinary commercial data mishandling — PECA's criminal provisions and general contract/tort law are the operative constraints today, but the company should build to the 2025 draft bill's likely requirements (notice, purpose limitation, retention limits, breach notice) now, both because the law may pass during the business's life and because UK/US/UAE clients will increasingly expect GDPR-adjacent practices regardless of Pakistani law.
- **Disclaimers/engagement letters**: essential and low-risk to implement. Every piece of "information" (as opposed to advice given by a named, retained advocate) should carry a clear "this is general information, not legal advice, and does not create an advocate-client relationship" disclaimer; every paid engagement should have a written engagement letter clarifying which entity (platform vs named advocate) is providing which service, for which fee, and that the platform is not a law firm and does not guarantee any outcome with police/courts/NADRA/FBR. This is standard, uncontroversial practice for any legal-adjacent platform and does not require further legal research to justify.

## 6. Practical structuring options — frank risk assessment

| # | Structure | Legal risk (officer) | Legal risk (spouse/company) | Reputational risk if it becomes public | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Officer is an equity holder / director / promoter of record** (even a small %, even via nominee/benami) | **High.** Direct breach of Conduct Rules r.15/16 and Police Order 2002 Art.117 (no sanction realistically obtainable); if paid work touches FIR/police/court matters, squarely in PPC 161-165 / Corruption Act s.5(1)(b)/(d) / NAB s.9 territory; concealed/nominee shareholding adds Corruption Act s.5-C (disproportionate assets) and NAB asset-declaration exposure. | High — the company itself becomes evidence in any corruption case against the officer; frozen/confiscated assets possible under NAB. | **Severe.** "Serving SP secretly owns FIR-fixing company" is a career-ending, potentially criminal-charge-triggering story; guaranteed viral/media interest given the police-corruption beat in Pakistani press. | **Do not do this under any structuring.** No amount of paperwork removes clause (d)'s "abusing his position… obtains… any valuable thing" or Art.117's blanket bar. |
| 2 | **"Sleeping partner" — officer takes an informal, undocumented profit-share (cash, gifts, favours) but is on no paperwork** | **High**, arguably *higher* than #1 in enforcement terms once discovered — undocumented cash flows to a public servant are precisely what PPC 161/163 and NAB s.9(a)(iv) are designed to catch, and there is no legitimate ownership record to point to as a defence; looks like classic bribery-in-instalments if investigated. | Same as #1 once linked. | **Severe**, and harder to defend publicly than #1 because there is no clean paper trail to show good faith — it looks like deliberate concealment. | The "informal partner" framing does not reduce legal risk; it removes only the paperwork, not the underlying conduct that the law criminalises, and destroys the ability to show good-faith reliance on sanction/disclosure processes. |
| 3 | **Spouse is 100% owner/director; officer has zero equity, zero role, zero pay, but is known within the family/community to be "behind" it** | **Medium-High** for the officer if the business's client-facing marketing or word-of-mouth trades on his rank/position (this alone can be read as "obtaining… pecuniary advantage" for a person related to him, per NAB s.9(a)(iv) and PPC 163's "personal influence" language, even with no formal ownership) — but meaningfully lower than #1/#2 if the marketing, brand, and client interactions never reference him and the spouse's business genuinely does not touch his police duties (i.e., focuses on land/tax/NADRA/company registration/errands and refers all FIR/police-specific matters to the independent advocates with no officer involvement at all). | Medium — spouse must independently satisfy her own Conduct Rules obligations (Rule 12 asset/income declaration must include this business; her own department's conduct rules on private trade/business apply to her exactly as they apply to him). | **High** if it ever surfaces that the "silent partner" is a serving SP, even with clean paperwork — Pakistani media and anti-corruption bodies do not require formal ownership to run a "cop's wife runs FIR-fixing business" story; but materially lower than #1/#2 because there is a genuine legal ownership separation to point to. | This is the most realistic starting point **only if the officer is genuinely and permanently walled off from any FIR/police/court-adjacent service line**, has no financial dependency on the business's success, and the business is marketed on the strength of the lawyer relatives' professional credentials, not the officer's rank. |
| 4 | **Officer is a genuinely unpaid, informal adviser only** (no equity, no salary, no consulting fee, no role in client-facing work, no mention in any marketing) — spouse and lawyer relatives own/run/front the business entirely | **Low-Medium.** Still not risk-free: even unpaid advice from a serving officer to a private company that profits from police-adjacent work can be framed as "showing favour… in the exercise of his official functions" if it ever demonstrably influenced a real case outcome, and Conduct Rules r.16(1)'s bar on "any employment or work" is not expressly limited to *paid* work — but absent any money or documented case-specific interference, there is no gratification for PPC 161-165/NAB s.9 to attach to, which removes the corruption-statute exposure that dominates options 1-3. | Low-Medium — ordinary company/tax/Bar-Council compliance risk only (§§3-5 above), same as any other founder-run advisory business. | **Medium.** If it surfaces that a serving SP is "advising" a business that profits from FIR/police help for a fee, it is still an awkward story, but a materially more defensible one ("no ownership, no pay, just informal family guidance — like any relative might give") than #1-3. | **This is the only option that is close to clean from a strict-compliance standpoint**, but it depends entirely on iron discipline: the officer must never be named/implied in marketing, never take a cut (formally or informally), never intervene in an actual client's case, and never let the business's police/FIR service line become a channel for his own department's business to reach him. |
| 5 | **Business deliberately excludes all police/FIR/criminal-matter services; officer has zero role of any kind** — company only does land, tax, NADRA, company registration, and non-police errands; police/FIR queries are declined or referred out to independent advocates with no officer connection at all | **Very Low** for the officer — nothing in the business touches his official function, so Conduct Rules r.14/15/16 exposure shrinks to the ordinary "does he have an undeclared outside interest at all" question (still requires disclosure of the spouse's shareholding on his asset return, and he must have literally no role), and PPC/NAB exposure requires some nexus to his office that this design removes. | Low — ordinary compliance risk only. | **Low.** No obvious "cop's family runs FIR-fixing company" story exists if the company visibly, provably never touches police/FIR work. | **This is the lowest-risk commercially-viable design**, at the cost of giving up the single most differentiated (and most monetisable, because overseas Pakistanis' single biggest pain point is often exactly FIR/police harassment) service line. |

## 7. Safe operating design — recommendation

Given the risk table above, the only designs that are not effectively prohibited are **#4 and #5**, and a hybrid of the two is the recommended target state:

1. **The officer holds no equity, directorship, or paid role in the company or any holding entity, foreign or domestic — full stop, for as long as he is serving.** This is not merely the safest option; it is very likely the *only* option that avoids PPC 161–165, Corruption Act s.5, and NAB s.9 exposure, given how broadly clause (d) ("by otherwise abusing his position… obtains… any pecuniary advantage") and s.9(a)(iv)/(vi) (benefit to "his spouse" or "any other person related to him") are drafted, and given Police Order 2002 Art.117's unqualified bar.
2. **Ownership sits with the spouse and the lawyer relatives**, on ordinary, fully-documented SECP paperwork (a normal Pvt Ltd, not a single-member structure that concentrates ownership in a way that invites the "who's really behind this" question). The spouse must independently comply with her own Conduct Rules — including declaring the shareholding and income on her own asset return — exactly as any civil servant with an outside business interest would.
3. **The FIR/police-complaint/police-conduct advisory line should either be dropped entirely (Option 5), or handled exclusively by the independent advocate relatives on their own professional letterhead and their own direct client billing, with the company doing nothing more than the ordinary case-intake/document/scheduling work it does for land, tax, NADRA, and company-registration matters** — i.e., no different treatment, no special channel, and critically, **no reference anywhere in marketing, sales calls, or client communications to the officer's rank, service, or ability to "help" with police matters.** The moment marketing trades on "we have a police officer on our team," the business has re-created the exact influence-peddling fact pattern regulators and journalists are primed to catch (see the FIA/FBR enforcement examples in §§1-2).
4. **Legal advice is always delivered by, invoiced by, and legally attributable to a named, independently retained advocate — never bundled into or resold as part of the company's own fee** (Bar Council Rule 135/136 touting/fee-splitting risk, §3). The company's own revenue model should be built around information/document/case-tracking/concierge-errand fees, not a cut of legal fees.
5. **Build the payment/tax stack around the ordinary Pakistani-startup playbook** — PSEB registration if the classification genuinely fits (confirm with PSEB/a tax adviser rather than assuming), a UK or US entity for card-payment access if needed, Payoneer/Wise as the core diaspora payout rail, and SBP's new lighter-touch export-proceeds rules — none of which are affected by the officer's status one way or the other, provided he holds no interest anywhere in the corporate group.
6. **Put engagement letters, "not legal advice" disclaimers, and basic PECA-grade data security (encryption, access controls, staff NDAs) in place from day one** — cheap, uncontroversial, and materially reduces both PECA exposure and ordinary client-dispute risk.
7. **Do not attempt to obtain "sanction" for the officer's involvement as a workaround.** Given the business's core value proposition touches his own department's work, an honest sanction application would likely be refused, and the application itself would flag the venture internally before it ever launches. The clean path is exclusion, not permission.

If, after all this, the family's real goal is to make the officer's professional network/reputation *the* differentiator rather than simply "connections in case something goes wrong" — that goal is close to structurally incompatible with him remaining a serving officer. The honest, low-risk version of this business is one where the officer's police background is family history, not a product feature, until he retires.
