## 2. Government Channels — What Exists, and How Well It Works

### 2.1 Overseas Pakistanis Commission (OPC), Punjab
- **Legal basis:** Punjab Overseas Pakistanis Commission Act, 2014 (passed by Punjab Assembly 24-Oct-2014, gazetted 10-Nov-2014) — a body to "receive and redress the grievances of overseas Pakistanis relating to Government Agencies," refer complaints to relevant offices, and recommend disciplinary/anti-corruption action against non-responsive public servants. A re-enactment, the Punjab Overseas Pakistanis Commission Act 2021, also exists. [Punjab Laws — Act 2014](http://punjablaws.gov.pk/laws/2574.html); [Punjab Laws — Act 2021](http://punjablaws.gov.pk/laws/2795.html); [Pakistan Lawyer summary](https://pakistanlawyer.com/punjab-overseas-pakistanis-commission-act-2014/)
- **Mandate (per OPC's own site):** welfare/reintegration support, redress of grievances within provincial jurisdiction, preventing land grabbing, supporting investment, feeding policy improvements back from complaint data. [OPC Punjab — Overview](https://opc.punjab.gov.pk/overview)
- **Complaint volumes/categories:**
  - 6,803 complaints filed under "Land Grabbing/Qabza Mafia" category in 2021–2022; 5,752 (85%) resolved by retrieving illegally occupied land; within Punjab specifically, 3,964 of 4,576 resolved (87%). [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2022/02/28/citizen-portal-resolved-85-of-land-related-complaints-of-overseas-pakistanis-in-2021/)
  - Cumulative portal figures cited in 2025 reporting: 7,814 complaints lodged, 3,579 resolved, rest under process; government claims >8,000 complaints resolved in two years with >Rs 100 billion in property recovered from land grabbers. [Express Tribune — "Expat investors bedeviled by property fraud"](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2581682/expat-investors-bedeviled-by-property-fraud)
  - Earlier historical claim: OPC found 10,000 valid complaints, helping ~5,000 overseas Pakistanis recover land or money **[unverified — no primary source found, cited secondhand]**.
- **Complaint filing:** Online via [OSPC Punjab portal](https://ospc.punjab.gov.pk/), Android app "Overseas Pakistani's Complaint" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=pk.pitb.gov.opc&hl=en)), built by PITB. [PITB — OPC](https://pitb.gov.pk/opc)
- **Assessment:** Resolution rates reported by government (~85%) sound strong, but these are self-reported by the same agency handling complaints, no independent audit found, and "resolved" isn't defined (does it mean possession returned, or merely case forwarded to police/revenue department?). Numbers are also small relative to the diaspora (a few thousand complaints/year against ~9 million overseas Pakistanis).

### 2.2 Overseas Pakistanis Foundation (OPF) — federal
- Statutory welfare body under the Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis and Human Resource Development. Runs a **Complaint Cell** that takes complaints via post, e-mail, fax, an Online Complaints Management System, and in-person visits, or via nearest Pakistani mission abroad. Explicitly free of cost, and warns against fraudulent actors impersonating OPF and asking for payment. [OPF — Complaint Cell](https://opf.org.pk/service/complaint-cell/); [OPF — Public Notice](https://opf.org.pk/public-notice-regarding-opf-complaint-portal/)
- Contact: e-mail/WhatsApp, phone +92 51 111040040. No published resolution-rate statistics were found for OPF specifically (distinct from OPC Punjab numbers above) — **[unverified]** whether OPF publishes comparable stats.

### 2.3 Pakistan Citizen's Portal (PCP) — PM's Performance Delivery Unit
- Nationwide grievance-redress mobile/web app (Android + iOS) with "special emphasis on facilitation of overseas Pakistanis." [MOFA](https://mofa.gov.pk/48143-2)
- Resolved >85% of land-related complaints from overseas Pakistanis in 2021, with >6,800 "Land Grabbing/Qabza Mafia" complaints filed and >5,700 resolved that year. [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2022/02/28/citizen-portal-resolved-85-of-land-related-complaints-of-overseas-pakistanis-in-2021/)
- OPF and OPC data appear to draw from/overlap with PCP figures — the "6,803–6,800" and "85%" numbers recur across sources, suggesting **one underlying dataset is being recycled by multiple press releases**, not three independent success stories.

### 2.4 Police complaint cells for overseas Pakistanis
- **Punjab Police:** WhatsApp helpline (0331-7871787) covering crime reporting, IG complaint cell access, and "Facilities for Overseas Pakistanis," positioned so expats don't have to travel to Pakistan for documents/action. Separate physical "Overseas Police Help Desks" established at District Police Officer (DPO) offices (e.g., DPO Gujrat). IGP Complaint Center short code 1787 for SMS/voice complaints. [Punjab Police](https://punjabpolice.punjab.gov.pk/node/11223); [DPO Gujrat](https://dpogjt.punjabpolice.gov.pk/overseas_police_help_desk)
- **Islamabad Police:** Dedicated "Overseas Pakistanis' Complaints Cell" (OPCC) launched 17-Sep-2022 by IGP Islamabad, staffed by a DSP; expanded in 2023 into a joint cell with the district administration and CDA for direct complaint routing; also separate CDA and Citizens Facilitation Centre support desks. [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2023/09/14/islamabad-police-launches-dedicated-complaint-cell-for-overseas-pakistanis/)
- **FIA:** Runs its own "Overseas Pakistani Complaint Cell." [FIA](https://www.fia.gov.pk/overseas)
- **Sindh:** No dedicated standalone cell found; the AIGP (Operations) Karachi is the designated focal point for overseas complaints, routed through the Sindh High Court's overseas-Pakistanis email channel. [Sindh High Court](https://sindhhighcourt.gov.pk/overseas_pakistanis.php)
- **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa:** All Additional Deputy Commissioners serve as focal persons for overseas complaints (no single dedicated commission found, unlike Punjab's OPC). [search result summary, GNQ Legal](https://gnqlegal.com/overseas-pakistanis/)
- **Assessment:** coverage exists in principle across all provinces, but it is fragmented (different agency per province, no single national single sign-on), mostly WhatsApp/hotline/email based rather than case-tracked, and Punjab is clearly the most institutionally mature (its own Act, its own portal, its own reported stats) versus Sindh/KP which rely on generic administrative focal points.

### 2.5 Illegal Dispossession Act, 2005
- Original law: criminalizes forcible/illegal dispossession of immovable property by "property grabbers," separate from ordinary civil suits, meant to give faster relief than a normal civil suit. [Khalid Zafar & Associates — Illegal Dispossession Act 2005](https://khalidzafar.com/laws-of-pakistan/illegal-dispossession-act-2005/); [Punjab Judicial Academy](https://pja.gov.pk/illegaldispossessionact)
- A search for a **specific "2023 amendment" targeting overseas Pakistanis could not be verified** — no primary legislative text or credible news report was found describing a 2023 amendment to this specific Act. **[unverified]** — what *was* found instead is a wave of entirely new, purpose-built legislation in 2024–2025 (below), which appears to be the mechanism policymakers actually used rather than amending the 2005 Act.

### 2.6 New special courts for overseas Pakistanis' property (2024–2025) — the most significant recent development
- **Federal:** *Establishment of Special Court (Overseas Pakistanis Property) Act, 2024* — passed by the Senate (Oct 2024) and National Assembly, sponsored by Senator Chaudhry Salik Hussain; jurisdiction covers Islamabad Capital Territory. Courts must decide petitions within 90 days; overseas Pakistanis can e-file petitions, submit evidence via video link (supervised by Pakistan's embassy/high commission/consulate abroad), and pay court fees electronically. Two District & Sessions Judges plus one Islamabad High Court judge have been designated. [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40353415/property-disputes-of-overseas-pakistanis-special-courts-being-set-up-in-islamabad); [MOFA press release](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/chaudhry-salik-hussains-bill-on-special-court-for-overseas-pakistanis-property-disputes-gets-senate-and-national-assembly-unanimous-approval); [Pakistan Code — full text](https://pakistancode.gov.pk/english/index.php/UY2FqaJw1-apaUY2Fqa-apaUY2Npa5pqag==-sg-jjjjjjjjjjjjj)
- **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa:** Adopted the same framework — KP government approved establishment of its own Special Court for Overseas Pakistanis under the 2024 federal Act template. [OPF](https://opf.org.pk/establishment-of-a-special-court-for-overseas-pakistanis-in-kpk/)
- **Punjab:** *Punjab Establishment of Special Courts (Overseas Pakistanis Property) Act 2025* — provincial equivalent, fast-track resolution, digital accessibility, e-filing, secure procedures, enforcement mechanisms. [Moiz Law Firm](https://moizlawfirm.com/overseas-pakistani-property-act-2025-special-courts-for-overseas-pakistanis-facing-property-disputes/); [Adalat Online — full text](https://www.adalatonline.pk/laws/overseas-pakistanis-property-act-2025/)
- These are new as of 2024–2026 and represent the government's clearest, most current legislative response to the exact problem this project targets. They are too new for reliable throughput/success-rate data to exist yet — no news source reports actual case outcomes from these special courts. **[unverified — outcomes]**

### 2.7 Adjacent bureaucratic channels relevant to the target problem set
- **FBR / non-resident tax filing:** For Tax Year 2026, IRIS removed the dedicated non-resident tax-return form that previously existed for overseas Pakistanis with no Pakistan-source income; only a generic "Resident/Non-Resident" toggle remains, creating confusion about correctly preserving Active Taxpayer List (filer) status and its associated lower withholding rates on property/vehicle transactions. No official FBR clarification/SRO was found as of the reporting date. [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40440429/missing-non-resident-form-overseas-pakistanis-face-tax-filing-issues)
- **NADRA succession certificates (wirasat/inheritance):** Overseas heirs can apply via NICOP/POC, with biometric verification completable at Pakistani missions abroad; NADRA publishes a public notice and waits 14 days for objections. **Critically, NADRA can only issue a certificate for undisputed inheritance** — if even one heir objects or family consensus breaks down, NADRA terminates the case and refers it to civil court, i.e., NADRA is not a dispute-resolution channel, only an uncontested-succession fast path. The 2025 Overseas Pakistanis Property Act allows an aggrieved overseas heir to file directly in the new Special Overseas Courts (90–180 day disposal target) instead. [aiksol360 guide](https://aiksol360.com/guide/inheritance-property-transfer-guide-pakistan/); [GNS Law Associates](https://gnslawassociates.com/succession-certificate-for-overseas-pakistanis/)
- **SECP company registration:** Fully digitized eServices/e-Zfile portal lets NICOP-holding Non-Resident Pakistanis (NRPs) become directors/shareholders remotely using passport/NICOP in lieu of CNIC; no mandatory resident-director requirement for a standard private limited company; capital repatriation needs separate SBP coordination. [Sterling Consultancy](https://sterling.pk/how-online-company-registration-works-for-overseas-pakistanis/)
- **HEC degree attestation:** Fully online since recent digitization (eservices.hec.gov.pk), 24/7 submission, downloadable e-certificate, no office visit/courier required; chain is University → HEC → MOFA → Embassy or Apostille (Pakistan is an Apostille Convention member, shortening the chain for member-country destinations). [HEC](https://www.hec.gov.pk/english/services/students/DAS/Pages/Degree-Attestation.aspx); [HCM Global](https://hcmglobalgroup.com/hec-attestation-pakistan-2026-digital-system-guide/)
- **Court video-link infrastructure:** Supreme Court and Islamabad High Court now hold multi-city hearings using video link and digital case files (e.g., linking Islamabad, Peshawar, Lahore, Karachi, Quetta, Mingora); an "Overseas Portal" keeps overseas litigants connected to case status; overseas witnesses/plaintiffs can record statements at Pakistani embassies/consulates under Oath Act 1873 supervision. [Supreme Court of Pakistan](https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk/video-link-case-hearing/); [App.com.pk](https://www.app.com.pk/national/sc-conducts-multi-city-hearing-using-video-link-and-digital-case-files/)

### Overall government-channel assessment
Government infrastructure is **broad on paper, fragmented in practice, and self-reported on performance**. Punjab is the most mature (its own Act, its own portal, its own — albeit repeated/recycled — statistics). The 2024–2025 Special Courts are the single most relevant new development but are unproven (no outcome data yet). FBR's non-resident filing regression in TY2026 shows execution can regress even as new courts are created. There is no single national case-tracking system spanning police + revenue + NADRA + FBR + courts for one complainant's issue — a citizen with a property-cum-inheritance-cum-FIR problem must navigate OPC/OPF/PCP + police + NADRA + civil/special court as separate silos.

### Payment-rail reality for any private player
- **PayPal does not work** for Pakistani residents/businesses (SBP restrictions on CNIC-based accounts and local withdrawal), which structurally blocks the most diaspora-familiar payment rail. [Wise blog](https://wise.com/pk/blog/paypal-account-in-pakistan)
- **Wise** works and is commonly recommended (e.g., ~$6.29 cost on a $1,000 transfer), but requires the recipient (law firm/service) to actively set up and manage a Wise account. [Wise blog](https://wise.com/pk/blog/receive-international-payments-in-pakistan)
- **Easypaisa/JazzCash** are domestic-only mobile wallets — cannot receive foreign currency directly. [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/best-ways-to-receive-international-payments-in-pakistan-beyond-paypal/)
- **Direct bank wire transfer** remains the fallback for larger payments. [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/best-ways-to-receive-international-payments-in-pakistan-beyond-paypal/)
- None of the law-firm websites reviewed published a specific payment mechanism publicly — payment terms appear to be negotiated privately per client, which is itself a friction point (and an opportunity: a productized service with transparent, prepaid, card-based pricing would stand out).

### Competitive-landscape summary
- The private market is **dense but fragmented and artisanal**: a long tail of Pakistan-based law firms (mostly Islamabad/Lahore/Karachi) that have bolted an "overseas Pakistanis" landing page onto a general litigation practice, plus a handful of UK-based solicitor firms serving the same UK diaspora from the regulated end.
- **None found were built as a productized, fixed-price, single-workflow digital platform** for the specific overseas-Pakistani legal/bureaucratic problem set — they are all still "contact us for a quote" traditional law-firm intake, or (in InstaKin's case) a general concierge/errand marketplace that touches attestation/paperwork but not litigation or land disputes.
- Real-estate platforms (Zameen, Graana, Hum Zameen, One Homes) solve *transactional* property needs (buying/managing) but explicitly do not solve *adversarial* problems (someone already occupying your land, a relative who won't relinquish a POA, a fraudulent sale).
- **Gap identified:** no single well-known brand today combines (a) transparent fixed pricing, (b) diaspora-friendly payment (card/Wise, not "contact us"), (c) case-status visibility/tracking, and (d) coverage spanning the full workflow (FIR → OPC/police → special court filing → NADRA/succession → FBR/company registration) rather than one practice area at a time.

## 4. Problem Categories — What Overseas Pakistanis Actually Complain About

| Category | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Land grabbing / illegal possession ("Qabza")** — including by relatives | Largest single complaint category on both OPC Punjab and Pakistan Citizen's Portal: 6,803–6,800 complaints in 2021–22 alone, ~85-87% claimed resolution rate. ## 6. Key Takeaways for Market Sizing / Opportunity

1. **Addressable population:** ~9 million overseas Pakistanis, concentrated in 5 markets (Saudi Arabia, UK, UAE, USA, Canada) that between them likely account for the large majority of the diaspora and of remittance flows.
2. **Money already flowing back:** >$41.6bn/year in remittances (FY2026) and >$12.7bn cumulative in Roshan Digital Account investment — demonstrating both financial capacity and an existing willingness to transact formally with Pakistan from abroad.
3. **Government response is real but young and fragmented:** the 2024–2025 wave of Special Courts (federal + KP + Punjab) is the strongest signal that this problem is large and politically salient enough to warrant new law — but these courts have no track record yet, and the surrounding ecosystem (OPC/OPF/police cells/NADRA/FBR) remains a patchwork of province-specific, self-reported, non-integrated channels.
4. **Private-sector response is a long tail of traditional law firms**, not a productized platform — pricing is opaque everywhere, payment rails are a real point of friction (no PayPal, Easypaisa/JazzCash domestic-only), and no single competitor spans the full problem workflow end-to-end.
5. **Highest-frequency, highest-pain problem categories** are consistently: land grabbing/illegal possession, POA abuse (often by relatives), inheritance/wirasat disputes the moment they become contested, and slow/opaque ordinary civil courts — with FBR non-resident filing status as a newer (2026) and currently unresolved pain point.
