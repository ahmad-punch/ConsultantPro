## Who this is for

You live abroad and hold, or plan to hold, property, a vehicle, a bank account or investments in Pakistan. You want to know whether you need to be on the Pakistani tax system and how to stay on the filer list while you are not living there.

## What the problem usually is

Three terms matter.

- **NTN.** National Tax Number. It is your registration with the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR).
- **ATL.** Active Taxpayer List. A public list of people who have filed returns. Being on it usually means lower withholding tax on bank transactions, property transfers and vehicle purchases. Being off it usually means higher rates.
- **Non-resident.** Tax residence depends on days spent in Pakistan in the tax year and other tests in section 82 of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001. Spending 183 days or more is generally treated as resident status, but other tests exist. Ask a tax adviser to apply them to you.

The current problem is a system change. For Tax Year 2026, reporting says the FBR's IRIS portal dropped the dedicated non-resident return form. Only a generic resident or non-resident toggle remained. Overseas filers were left unsure how to keep ATL status, and no FBR clarification had been issued when it was reported ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40440429/missing-non-resident-form-overseas-pakistanis-face-tax-filing-issues)).

Some tax blogs say a simplified electronic return for non-residents with no Pakistan-source income was introduced by a 2025 notice. We could not confirm that against an FBR notice. Do not rely on it until you have checked IRIS or a tax adviser.

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **FBR**, through the IRIS portal (iris.fbr.gov.pk). Registration, returns and ATL status all sit here.
- **A registered tax practitioner or chartered accountant** can file on your behalf.
- **Banks, sub-registrars and vehicle registration offices** apply the withholding rate by checking the ATL.

## The pathway

1. **Count your days.** Count days in Pakistan during the tax year, which runs 1 July to 30 June. Keep passport stamps and tickets.
2. **List Pakistan-source income.** Rent, bank profit, dividends, capital gains, business income. Also list assets, such as land and bank accounts.
3. **Check whether you already have an NTN.** Many people do from an earlier bank or property transaction. IRIS shows this by CNIC or NICOP.
4. **Register if you do not.** Registration is online. You generally need your CNIC or NICOP and a working mobile number for one-time passwords. Overseas applicants sometimes struggle with the mobile number requirement. Ask a practitioner how they handle it.
5. **Check the current return route on IRIS** for your residency status. Because of the Tax Year 2026 form issue, confirm which form or option applies to you before you file.
6. **File the return** by the deadline. Reports on the Tax Year 2026 deadline conflict. One consultancy blog says it was extended to 15 October 2026. If so, it has passed. Check the FBR site for any later notice.
7. **Check the ATL.** Search your CNIC or NTN on the FBR's ATL page after filing. If you are missing, ask the practitioner what to do.
8. **Keep records** of the return, the acknowledgement and any notice.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **Registration and filing.** Often a few days with all documents ready. We do not have a verified average.
- **ATL update.** The list updates on a schedule set by FBR. We do not have a verified frequency.
- **Late filing.** A June 2026 report said a late filer might pay a surcharge of Rs 25,000 to get back on the ATL ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/28/file-your-tax-returns-on-time-or-pay-fbr-rs-25000-fine/)). The report predates the deadline extension. Confirm the rule in the current Finance Act.
- **Practitioner fees.** Pakistan filing services publish prices from about PKR 3,900 for a basic return to PKR 14,500 for a one-to-one consultant call ([Befiler](https://www.befiler.com/)). Prices change. Complex returns cost more.

## Traps to avoid

- **Assuming non-resident means no filing.** Having assets or Pakistan-source income can still require a return.
- **Sharing your IRIS password and OTP** with an agent you do not know. Use an authorised practitioner and change the password afterwards.
- **Filing as resident by mistake** because the form option is missing. This can create wrong tax liabilities.
- **Waiting until a sale.** Buyers and registrars check the ATL at the time of the deal. Fixing it then costs more.
- **Ignoring FBR notices** sent to an old email or phone.
- **Believing anyone who promises a particular tax outcome.**
