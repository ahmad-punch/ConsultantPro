## Who this is for

You live abroad and want to buy a plot, a house, a flat or farmland in Pakistan. Or you want to check a purchase you have already made. You may be using a relative, a dealer or a housing scheme as your contact on the ground.

## What the problem usually is

Buying from afar means you cannot walk the land or sit in the registrar's office. You rely on other people's words and papers. That is where the losses happen.

Reported patterns include forged sale papers, plots sold twice, and sales by people who do not own the land. One law firm's website says about 25 percent of overseas property disputes involve fake sales or forged documents. That is a firm's figure, not an independent survey, but the risk is real ([Pakistan Legal Services](https://pakistanlegalservices.com/overseas-pakistani-property-disputes/)). One Express Tribune report describes a land-file fraud of Rs 210 million ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2581682/expat-investors-bedeviled-by-property-fraud)).

Many buyers also lose money in the early stage, when they pay a token or advance before the papers are checked.

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **The revenue office** holds land records. The key record is the fard, an extract showing who is recorded as owner. A mutation (intiqal) is the entry that records a change of owner. In Punjab, land records are also available through digital land record centres.
- **The sub-registrar** registers the sale deed (registry).
- **Development authorities and housing societies** approve schemes and keep their own transfer records.
- **FBR** affects tax: your filer status changes the withholding tax on a purchase. See our FBR guide.
- **Your bank** moves the money.
- **An independent advocate** checks title and drafts or reviews the agreement.

## The pathway

1. **Decide the type.** Agricultural land, a plot in an approved scheme, an existing house, or a flat. Each has its own checks.
2. **Collect the seller's papers.** Registry deed, fard, mutation, CNIC, and for a scheme, allotment letter and the society's transfer papers.
3. **Trace the chain of title.** Who owned it before the seller? Did each transfer happen through a registry or a recorded mutation? A gap is a warning.
4. **Verify at the source.** Have your own independent person or advocate check the record at the revenue office or the society, not just look at copies the seller supplies.
5. **Check the scheme approval.** For a plot in a scheme, check that the relevant authority has approved the scheme, not only that the dealer says so.
6. **Check the physical plot.** Visit, photograph, and speak to neighbours. Confirm nobody else occupies it. See our guide on illegal occupation for what that can look like later.
7. **Sign a written agreement to sell** only after the checks. It should name the parties, the property, the price, the payment schedule and the deadline for transfer.
8. **Pay in stages through a bank,** to the seller's own named account, against documents. Keep every receipt.
9. **Complete the registry and mutation** in your name. The sale is not finished until the record shows you.
10. **Collect possession** and keep a copy of every document.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **Verification.** A few days to weeks, depending on the office and the chain of title. We do not have a verified average.
- **Registry and mutation.** Depends on province, office and property type. We do not have reliable figures.
- **Taxes and fees.** Stamp duty, registration fees and withholding taxes depend on province, property value and your filer status. We do not quote a figure. Ask a tax adviser or the registrar.
- **Remote purchase schemes.** Some banks market remote investment and housing finance for overseas Pakistanis ([DIB Pakistan](https://www.dibpak.com/index.php/roshan-digital-account/roshan-apna-ghar/)). Read the terms. Bank products do not check a private seller's title for you.
- **Advocate fees.** Vary. Ask for a written quote.

## Traps to avoid

- **Token money (bayana) before checks.** A small payment often turns into a pressure point and a lost deposit.
- **Paying cash or into a dealer's personal account.**
- **Trusting photocopies.** Documents must be checked at the issuing office.
- **"File" trading in housing schemes** where the scheme itself is not approved.
- **A seller who cannot appear** and sends a power of attorney. Verify the POA and the person.
- **Buying "cheap" from a relative's friend** with no papers.
- **Letting the seller keep the title papers** after you pay.
- **Skipping your filer check.** A non-filer buyer can pay higher withholding tax.
- **Pressure to decide today.**
