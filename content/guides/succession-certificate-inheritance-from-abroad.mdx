## Who this is for

You live outside Pakistan. A parent, spouse or other close relative has died. They left land, a house, a bank account or other assets in Pakistan. You are one of the legal heirs and you want your share recorded properly.

## What the problem usually is

A death does not move property to the heirs by itself on paper. The land record and the bank still show the dead person's name. Until that changes, nobody can sell, divide or borrow against the asset in a clean way.

The usual problems are these:

- One relative says they are the only heir, or they hold the land and say nothing is owed to anyone.
- Some heirs are left out, often daughters, a second wife or her children, or heirs who live abroad.
- The family agrees verbally and nothing is written down. Years pass. Then someone sells.

For Muslim families, shares in an estate are fixed by Islamic law. A will can usually cover only part of the estate. A lawyer should confirm the exact shares for your family. This guide does not calculate them.

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **NADRA** issues a succession certificate when the inheritance is undisputed. It is not a place to settle a fight.
- **The civil court** decides disputed inheritance. If even one heir objects, NADRA stops and the matter goes to court ([aiksol360](https://aiksol360.com/guide/inheritance-property-transfer-guide-pakistan/); [GNS Law Associates](https://gnslawassociates.com/succession-certificate-for-overseas-pakistanis/)).
- **Special courts for overseas Pakistanis' property** now exist under a federal Act of 2024 and a Punjab Act of 2025. Reporting says an aggrieved overseas heir may be able to use them. They are new and we found no outcome data ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40353415/property-disputes-of-overseas-pakistanis-special-courts-being-set-up-in-islamabad)).
- **The revenue office** (for land) or the **local development authority or housing society** (for urban plots) records the new owners after the certificate.
- **Pakistani missions abroad** take biometrics for heirs who cannot travel.

## The pathway

1. **Collect documents.** You will need the death certificate, the CNIC or NICOP of the dead person, and the CNIC, NICOP or passport of every heir. Collect any property papers you can find: registry deeds, fards, mutation numbers, bank statements.
2. **List every heir.** Write down spouse, sons, daughters, and living parents of the dead person. Check for earlier marriages. A missing heir can undo the whole process later.
3. **Start the application in Pakistan.** Reporting says one legal heir starts the application at a NADRA centre in Pakistan. A letter of authorisation from the other heirs is part of this. Reports say that since September 2025 any NADRA centre can take the application ([PakObserver](https://pakobserver.net/nadra-makes-succession-certificate-process-easier-check-free-from-september-2025/)).
4. **Give your biometrics abroad.** Heirs outside Pakistan can give biometric consent at a Pakistani mission's NADRA desk ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/national/expatriates-to-get-succession-certificates-abroad-through-biometrics/)). Some missions charge a service fee. One mission page lists NADRA's own fee for this step as nil and a consulate charge of $60 per application, but that page is undated ([Pakistan Consulate Houston](https://pakistanconsulatehouston.org/consular-services/nadra-facilitation-desk)). Check your own mission.
5. **Public notice.** NADRA publishes a notice. It then waits 14 days for objections.
6. **If nobody objects,** NADRA can issue the certificate. If someone objects, NADRA closes the file and you move to court.
7. **After the certificate: transfer.** The certificate is not the transfer. Take it to the revenue office, development authority or housing society to record the heirs' names. In land records this step is called mutation (intiqal). Banks have their own rules for releasing accounts.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **Undisputed NADRA route.** A mission counter in London was reported as aiming for processing within 15 days of the application ([Pakistan High Commission, London](https://new.phclondon.org/?p=435)). Treat that as a best case. Missing papers or record mismatches add weeks or months. We do not have a reliable average.
- **Disputed route.** Civil court cases about inheritance can run for years. The special courts have a 60 or 90 day target on paper. Reporting says the deadline "seems more like a suggestion" ([Jurist Panel](https://juristpanel.com/2025/04/14/protecting-the-rights-of-overseas-pakistanis-through-special-court-overseas-pakistanis-property-act-2024/)).
- **Costs.** Government and NADRA fees are small compared with property value, but we could not confirm a current official fee schedule for the certificate. Advocate fees vary by city and case. Ask for the fee in writing before you agree.

## Traps to avoid

- **Signing an authority for one sibling without limits.** A broad power of attorney lets that person act for you in many ways. See our guide on powers of attorney.
- **Agreeing to a "family settlement" you have not read.** Ask for the written draft and read it with someone independent.
- **Paying an agent in advance** to "get the certificate quickly." There is one official process. Pay official fees only through official channels.
- **Leaving out an heir** to keep the application simple. The omission can be used against you later.
- **Selling inherited land before mutation.** Most later disputes start here.
