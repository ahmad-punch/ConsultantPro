## Who this is for

You live abroad and want to start a business in Pakistan, or hold assets through a company. You may want to sell services, run a shop, hold property, or work with Pakistani partners. You hold a NICOP or a Pakistani passport.

## What the problem usually is

Registering is usually the easy part. SECP, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, has digital systems. Overseas Pakistanis with a NICOP can register remotely, using a passport or NICOP in place of a CNIC ([Sterling Consultancy](https://sterling.pk/how-online-company-registration-works-for-overseas-pakistanis/)).

The problems come after:

- Choosing the wrong company type for the goal.
- Not knowing what must be filed every year.
- A consultant registers the company in their own name or keeps the login.
- No bank account. Banks ask many questions of non-resident owners.
- Capital cannot easily be moved out. Currency approval and repatriation need separate coordination with the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP).
- Tax registration and filings are missed.

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **SECP** registers companies and holds the company record. Its eServices portal handles name reservation and incorporation.
- **FBR** issues the tax registration for the company and receives its returns.
- **A commercial bank** opens the company account.
- **State Bank of Pakistan** sets the rules on foreign exchange, investment inflows and repatriation.
- **PSEB** (Pakistan Software Export Board) registration may matter for IT service exporters. Check whether it fits your work.
- **A company secretary, accountant or advocate** can help with drafting and filings.

## The pathway

1. **Decide the structure.** A private limited company is common. Other forms exist, such as sole proprietorships and partnerships, with different rules. The right form depends on your goal. Ask an adviser.
2. **Check eligibility.** Confirm your NICOP is valid. No mandatory resident director is reported for a standard private limited company ([Sterling Consultancy](https://sterling.pk/how-online-company-registration-works-for-overseas-pakistanis/)). The minimum number of directors depends on the type of company. Check the current rule with SECP or an adviser.
3. **Choose and reserve a name.** Use the SECP eServices name search. Names can be rejected if they match or resemble others.
4. **Prepare the documents.** These include the company's constitutional documents and details of directors and shareholders. SECP lists the exact set.
5. **Submit online** and pay the fee. Keep the reference number.
6. **Receive the certificate of incorporation.**
7. **Register with FBR** for a company NTN, and with other bodies as needed, for example sales tax.
8. **Open a bank account.** Ask the bank in advance what it will require from non-resident directors.
9. **Plan the money.** If you send capital from abroad or plan to take profits out, ask the bank and a professional about SBP rules first.
10. **Set a compliance calendar** for annual filings and tax returns.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **SECP fee.** About PKR 30,000 for companies with local shareholders, and about PKR 40,000 where foreign shareholders are involved ([Tenco Consulting](https://tencoconsulting.com/company-registration-fee-calculator-pakistan/)). Fees depend on share capital and change over time.
- **Consultant fees.** Reported at PKR 5,000 to 10,000 or more as an add-on, or PKR 30,000 and up for full-service support ([Alrushd Law](https://alrushdlaw.com/secp-company-registration-fee/); [BACO Consultants](https://bacoconsultants.com/blogs/company-registration-cost-pakistan-2026)).
- **Registration time.** Often days when documents are ready. We have no verified average.
- **Bank account and SBP steps.** Often slower than registration, and we do not have reliable figures.
- **Annual compliance costs.** Depend on the company's size and the accountant. We did not verify a typical figure.

## Traps to avoid

- **The consultant keeps control.** The shares must be in your name and the portal login in your control. Never let someone else be the sole shareholder "for convenience."
- **Using a nominee director** you do not know.
- **Skipping the annual filings.** Penalties and strike-off risk follow.
- **Mixing personal and company money.**
- **Assuming money can leave freely.** Check SBP rules first.
- **Copying a template memorandum** without reading what it allows the company to do.
- **Paying an unlisted fee** to "speed up" registration. SECP fees are published.
