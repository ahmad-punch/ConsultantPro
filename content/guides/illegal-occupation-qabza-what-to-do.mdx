## Who this is for

You own land, a plot or a house in Pakistan. You live abroad. Someone else is now living on it, farming it, renting it out, or has built on it. Often that person is a relative or a former caretaker.

## What the problem usually is

Illegal occupation is called qabza. It is the most reported complaint type from overseas Pakistanis. In 2021 and 2022 the Citizen's Portal recorded 6,803 land grabbing complaints, and the government said 85 percent were resolved. That figure is self-reported, and "resolved" is not defined ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2022/02/28/citizen-portal-resolved-85-of-land-related-complaints-of-overseas-pakistanis-in-2021/)). Treat it as a sign the problem is common, not as a promise.

Common patterns:

- A relative who was trusted to look after the land refuses to leave or to pay.
- Someone uses forged papers to claim a sale or a gift. One law firm's website says about 25 percent of disputes involve fake sales or forged documents. That is a firm's own figure, not an independent survey ([Pakistan Legal Services](https://pakistanlegalservices.com/overseas-pakistani-property-disputes/)).
- A tenant stops paying and claims rights.

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **Overseas Pakistanis Commission (OPC), Punjab.** Takes complaints through the OSPC portal and an Android app ([OSPC](https://ospc.punjab.gov.pk/)).
- **Pakistan Citizen's Portal.** A national complaint app with a focus on overseas Pakistanis.
- **Overseas Pakistanis Foundation (OPF) Complaint Cell.** Free. OPF warns that people impersonate it and ask for money. Never pay to lodge an OPF complaint ([OPF](https://opf.org.pk/service/complaint-cell/)).
- **Police cells** for overseas complaints exist in Punjab, Islamabad and at the FIA. Sindh and KP use focal persons.
- **Court of Session** under the Illegal Dispossession Act 2005. This law treats forced dispossession as a criminal matter and aims at faster relief than an ordinary suit ([Khalid Zafar & Associates](https://khalidzafar.com/laws-of-pakistan/illegal-dispossession-act-2005/)).
- **Special courts** for overseas Pakistanis' property: a federal Act of 2024 for Islamabad, a Punjab Act of 2025, and a Khyber Pakhtunkhwa court. They are new. We found no outcome data.
- **Civil court.** A suit for possession, declaration or injunction.

**A warning on a recent Punjab law.** Punjab passed a Protection of Ownership of Immovable Property Act in 2025. It let deputy commissioner committees decide occupation disputes. In December 2025 the Lahore High Court suspended it, and in February 2026 reporting says a full bench set aside the committees' decisions and sent more than 1,500 petitions to tribunals ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1974630); [APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1167394)). We found no later report. Check the current status before relying on it.

## The pathway

1. **Collect proof of ownership.** Registry deed, fard (record of rights), mutation entry, inheritance papers, tax receipts. Get a fresh certified fard.
2. **Collect proof of the problem.** Dates, photos, messages, names of witnesses, utility bills in the occupier's name, any rent receipts.
3. **Check the record has not changed.** Confirm no sale or mutation has been entered against your name. A recent forged entry changes the route.
4. **Decide the goal.** Possession back, rent, a negotiated exit, or a sale. The goal shapes the route.
5. **Make an official complaint.** An OPC, Citizen's Portal or OPF complaint costs little and creates a record. It may prompt an inquiry. It is not a court order.
6. **Get a Pakistani advocate's view on the court route.** The main options are:
   - A complaint under the Illegal Dispossession Act, usually where occupation is by force or fraud.
   - A special court petition if you qualify as an overseas Pakistani under the Act.
   - A civil suit. In general, a person dispossessed without lawful process can ask a civil court for possession within a short window, often discussed as six months, regardless of title. Ask your advocate whether that window applies and whether it has passed.
   - A request for an interim order (injunction or status quo) so the occupier cannot sell or build while the case runs.
7. **Give a valid authority** (usually a power of attorney) to someone you trust, or arrange to give evidence by video link from a mission. See our power of attorney guide.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **Official complaints.** Weeks to months for a first response. We have no reliable average.
- **Special courts.** The statute sets a deadline of 60 to 90 days, but reporting says the timeframe "seems more like a suggestion than a deadline," and a favourable judgment does not by itself remove an occupier ([Jurist Panel](https://juristpanel.com/2025/04/14/protecting-the-rights-of-overseas-pakistanis-through-special-court-overseas-pakistanis-property-act-2024/)).
- **Civil suit.** Often years, plus a separate execution stage to enforce.
- **Costs.** Court fees depend on the value of the claim. Advocate fees vary. We do not have a reliable range. Ask for a written fee quote.

## Traps to avoid

- **Paying someone to "clear" the occupier.** Ask what lawful process they will use. If there is none, do not pay.
- **Self-help.** Sending people to remove an occupier can lead to criminal complaints against you.
- **Paying anyone to lodge a free government complaint.**
- **Delay.** The longer the occupier stays, the more they can claim and the harder evidence gets.
- **Signing a compromise** you have not had read by an independent advocate.
