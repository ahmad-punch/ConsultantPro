## Who this is for

You are abroad and you are a party in a civil case in Pakistan, or you are thinking of starting one. It may be about land, inheritance, a contract or money. You want to understand what happens, what to ask, and how long to plan for.

## What the problem usually is

Civil cases decide rights between people: who owns land, who owes money, who must hand over possession. Unlike a criminal case, nobody goes to prison. The court issues orders.

Overseas parties usually have three difficulties.

- They cannot follow what is happening and rely on a single relative or lawyer.
- They do not know why dates keep changing.
- They expect a decision in months and are surprised by years.

The government itself accepts that ordinary property litigation can be slow. The reason given for the new special courts for overseas Pakistanis, with 60 to 90 day targets, was "endless delays in civil courts" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2581682/expat-investors-bedeviled-by-property-fraud)).

## Who owns it (the office/forum)

- **The civil court of first instance.** For many property and money cases, this is the civil judge in the district where the property lies or the defendant lives. The court's power depends on the subject and the value.
- **Appeal courts.** District judges and the High Court, depending on the case.
- **Special courts** for overseas Pakistanis' property under the federal 2024 Act, the Punjab 2025 Act, and a KP court. They are new, with no outcome data yet.
- **Your advocate**, who files documents and appears on your behalf.
- **The court's cause list and case file**, which show the next date.

## The pathway

These stages are the usual order. Some cases skip some of them.

1. **Pre-case work.** Gather documents. Decide the remedy you want. A lawyer may send a legal notice first.
2. **Plaint.** Your advocate files the written claim, with court fee based on the value of the claim.
3. **Summons.** The court sends notice to the other side. Delivery problems cause many delays.
4. **Interim relief.** You may ask for a temporary order, such as a stay or a status quo order, so the other side cannot sell or build while the case runs. This is often the most important early step in property cases.
5. **Written statement.** The other side files its reply. Time limits exist but extensions are common.
6. **Issues.** The court lists the questions it must decide.
7. **Evidence.** Each side produces documents and witnesses. Witnesses are questioned and cross-examined. Overseas parties can often give evidence by video link supervised at a Pakistani mission ([Supreme Court of Pakistan](https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk/video-link-case-hearing/)). Ask your advocate whether this fits your case.
8. **Final arguments.** Advocates sum up.
9. **Judgment and decree.** The court decides and issues an order.
10. **Appeal or revision.** Either side may challenge the decision within set time limits.
11. **Execution.** A winning party often must file a separate application to enforce the decree, for example to recover possession. This can take long.

## What remedies exist

- **Declaration.** The court declares who owns the property or that a document is invalid.
- **Possession.** An order to hand back property.
- **Permanent injunction.** An order stopping the other side from an act.
- **Partition.** Division of jointly owned property among co-owners or heirs.
- **Cancellation of a document.** For example, a forged sale deed.
- **Money recovery and damages.**

## How to track a case from abroad

- **Ask your advocate for four things** after every hearing: the case number, the court's name, what happened, and the next date and purpose.
- **Check the cause list** if the court publishes it online. Your advocate can show where.
- **Ask for copies** of every order and filing, in writing.
- **Set a rhythm.** For example, a short written update after every hearing.
- **Keep a valid power of attorney** or arrange video participation, so the case does not stall. See our power of attorney guide.

## Realistic timelines and costs

- **Ordinary civil cases.** Often years from filing to a final decision, and more with appeals and execution. We do not have a reliable average for a particular court.
- **Special courts.** On paper, 60 to 90 days. Reporting says the timeframe "seems more like a suggestion than a deadline" ([Jurist Panel](https://juristpanel.com/2025/04/14/protecting-the-rights-of-overseas-pakistanis-through-special-court-overseas-pakistanis-property-act-2024/)).
- **Costs.** Court fee depends on the claim value. Advocate fees depend on city, court and stage. We do not have a verified range. A reported benchmark: a Pakistani High Court appeal can cost PKR 200,000 or more ([Saeed Law Firm](https://saeedlawfirm.com/blog/how-much-does-a-lawyer-cost-in-pakistan)). Ask for a written fee schedule by stage.

## Why cases take years

- Many cases per judge and many adjournments.
- Summons that are not served, or are served late.
- Requests for more time to file replies.
- Missing witnesses or documents.
- Appeals at several levels.
- A separate execution stage after the decree.

## Traps to avoid

- **A lawyer who promises a quick win.**
- **Missing your own steps,** such as signing documents or sending evidence.
- **Settling under pressure** without reading the terms.
- **Starting without checking the time limit.** Some claims lose their strength with delay. Ask early.
