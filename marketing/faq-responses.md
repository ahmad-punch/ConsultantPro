**1. What is my share in my father's property?**
For Muslim families, shares are fixed by Islamic law, and a lawyer should confirm the exact figures for your family. We can list the likely heirs and the documents needed, and explain the next steps. A Pathway Question ($15) gets you a written answer in 48 hours. Please send who survived him and what the estate includes.

**2. I live abroad. Can a Pakistani court still help me?**
Generally yes. Living abroad does not remove your rights. You usually act through an advocate and a valid power of attorney, and some courts allow evidence by video link. We cannot promise an outcome or a date. A 30-minute call ($49) can explain the route for your case. What is the dispute about?

**3. How do I get my father's property back from my brother?**
The right route depends on your papers and who holds the land. Options include an official complaint, a special court for overseas property, or a civil case. Each has different timelines. Please do not pay anyone who promises to clear the land. Send your documents and we will write you a plan (Case Assessment, $249).

**4. Who is the legal heir to a father's property?**
For Muslims, the main heirs are usually the spouse, children and sometimes parents. Daughters have shares too. The exact result depends on who survived and on any valid will. A lawyer should confirm. Send us the family details and we will map the heirs and the process. A written answer is $15.

**5. My father died in 2020. How do I transfer his property to my name from abroad?**
Usually two stages. First, a succession certificate, which NADRA issues if all heirs agree. Heirs abroad can give biometrics at a Pakistani mission. Second, mutation, which updates the land record. Do not sell before mutation. We can walk you through both. Our $49 call fits this well.

**6. We inherited jointly. How do we divide it and what documents do we need?**
Usually you need the death certificate, ID of every heir, and the property papers (registry, fard, mutation). Division can be agreed in writing or ordered by a court. Get independent advice before signing any family agreement. We can prepare a checklist and a plan for your advocate. Tell us what the property is.

**7. I am in the UK and need an affidavit for property in Pakistan. How?**
It depends on what the affidavit is for, and who in Pakistan will receive it. Documents signed abroad often need attestation at a Pakistani mission. An advocate should draft it for your purpose. Tell us the purpose and the office that asked for it, and we will explain the steps. A $15 question is a good start.

**8. Can I file an FIR from abroad, or must I fly back?**
You generally do not have to fly back. Complaints can go through an authorised relative or advocate, or through overseas complaint cells in some provinces and the FIA. We give general information only and do not contact police. An independent advocate advises on the case. Tell us briefly what happened and we will explain the process.

**9. How do I execute a valid power of attorney from abroad?**
Draft it narrowly with an advocate. Sign it in person at a Pakistani mission, or through the digital route where available. The attorney then registers it in Pakistan, often within a deadline your mission will state. Our guide on power of attorney covers the steps and the risks. Want us to check your plan? A $15 question works.

**10. Is it safe to give someone power of attorney?**
It can be, if you limit it. Use a special POA for one task, set an end date, require that money goes to your own account, and check the land record every few months. A broad POA to one person is the most common source of loss. We can review your plan in a $49 call.

**11. How do I become a tax filer from abroad?**
You register for an NTN with the FBR and file a return. Residency depends on days spent in Pakistan and other tests. For Tax Year 2026, reports say the dedicated non-resident form was missing on IRIS, so check the current route first. A tax practitioner can file for you. Ask us a $15 question and we will explain.

**12. How do I get my degree attested from abroad?**
The usual chain is university, then HEC, then the Foreign Ministry apostille or attestation. HEC is online. Check what the receiving body needs first. Government fees are small, but agent fees add up. We can run the whole errand. Our fee starts from $35, plus government fees at cost. Which document do you have?

**13. How do I renew my NICOP from abroad?**
Apply through NADRA's official online system or your nearest Pakistani mission. Check which route applies in your country. Fees depend on speed. Never share your NADRA login with an agent. Fake sites are common. We can handle the errand from $35 plus government fees. Tell us your country and when the card expires.

**14. I paid a "lawyer" and he stopped replying. What can I do?**
Please first gather the receipts, messages and any signed papers. Do not send more money. Options may include a written demand, a complaint to the Bar Council if he is an advocate, or a complaint to the FIA if it looks like fraud. An independent advocate should advise. We can organise your documents and explain the options (Pathway Call, $49).

**15. Where do I complain if my property was occupied?**
Official options include the Overseas Pakistanis Commission in Punjab, the Pakistan Citizen's Portal, and the OPF Complaint Cell. These are free. Never pay anyone to lodge them. Court routes also exist. Our guide on illegal occupation explains each. Send us your papers if you want a written plan.
